## Core idea

This workstream is adapting an adult communication and relationship book into a youth-facing version.
The current source coverage is research-heavy and focuses on what editorial choices are developmentally appropriate for teens.

## Main evidence

- adolescent neurodevelopment is treated as a design constraint for tone and explanation
- rejection sensitivity is a major interpretive lens for how youth readers receive criticism or guidance
- emotional self-reliance is reframed carefully to avoid glorifying isolation
- scenarios, scripts, and concrete situations are preferred over abstract moral instruction
- feedback should separate behavior from identity so the reader does not experience guidance as a global attack

## Key themes

- warm, autonomy-supportive tone over adult authority tone
- emotional scaffolding over diagnostic labeling
- developmental realism over moralizing
- editorial caution around shame, cynicism, and displaced aggression

## Current gap

The ingested materials mainly describe how the youth edition should be adapted.
They do not yet provide chapter-by-chapter rewritten manuscript evidence inside this wiki.

## Why this matters in the broader corpus

This project extends the broader worldview into a B2C editorial form:

- interpret experience before giving technique
- translate complex emotional life into language a reader can safely use
- maintain dignity while still being concrete and practical
