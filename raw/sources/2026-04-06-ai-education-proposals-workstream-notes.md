## Core idea

This workstream packages AI education into saleable proposal assets.
The central thesis is that managers and practitioners should not be trained as if they have the same leverage point.

## Main evidence

- the integrated proposal explicitly recommends separate manager and practitioner tracks
- the manager proposal focuses on direction, approval, feedback, decision quality, and organizational lead-time reduction
- the practitioner proposal focuses on grounding, prompt structure, document workflows, and daily execution speed
- both tracks emphasize hands-on work and reusable operating assets over conceptual overview sessions

## Key themes

- split operating logic by role, not just by tool familiarity
- convert AI education into concrete templates, dashboards, promptbooks, and decision artifacts
- measure outcomes in lead time, approval rate, feedback rounds, and template adoption
- connect classroom activity to a 30-day operating rhythm when role leverage justifies it

## Important nuance

Although there is an integrated proposal artifact, the document itself argues against collapsing the programs into a single undifferentiated course.
The portfolio is therefore better understood as a deliberate dual-track system, not a single unified curriculum.

## Why this matters in the broader corpus

This is the commercial interface of the broader education system:

- proposals translate worldview into purchase-ready language
- enterprise education is framed as operational redesign, not feature training
- assets created in proposals appear to feed later delivery and reusable templates
