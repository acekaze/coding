# Education product redesign through practice

## Summary

Several current workstreams treat learning not as abstract content transfer but as the redesign of a real working artifact through practice, feedback, and revision.

## Definition

A course follows this concept when the participant brings a real lecture, workflow, report, or operating problem into the room and leaves with an improved version that is closer to immediate use.

## Why it matters

This idea helps explain why the public course, proposal system, and instructional design materials all emphasize outputs, templates, and execution loops instead of generalized inspiration.

## Supporting ideas

- the public course asks participants to improve a representative lecture
- the proposal portfolio frames learning around logs, templates, approval flows, and measurable operating changes
- the instructional design workbench translates abstract capabilities into runnable class artifacts

## Tensions or caveats

- this model requires participants to bring enough real material into the learning process
- strong artifact focus can create scope creep if the target output is not clearly bounded
- the public course still shows unresolved format drift in the current document set
