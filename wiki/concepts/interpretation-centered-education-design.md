# Interpretation-centered education design

## Summary

Across the current corpus, education is framed less as technique transfer and more as the interpretation of experience into language, structure, action, and recovery.

## Definition

This concept describes a design stance where the educator first interprets the human, organizational, or emotional situation accurately and then builds teaching, guidance, or language from that understanding.

## Why it matters

It is the strongest candidate for the unifying logic that links the Jeon Jong-mok context, the public course, the youth book adaptation, and the instructional design workbench.

## Supporting ideas

- the Jeon Jong-mok context explicitly positions the work as interpretation rather than mere performance coaching
- the public course helps instructors reinterpret their own experience into a stronger teaching asset
- the instructional design guides translate abstract capabilities into teachable movement toward action
- the youth adaptation reframes adult language advice through adolescent developmental reality

## Tensions or caveats

- interpretation can become overreach if the wiki stops distinguishing evidence from later synthesis
- the source corpus is uneven, with some workstreams represented by worldview documents and others by operational drafts
